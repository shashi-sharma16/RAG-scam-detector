Scam Type: Phishing Scam

Warning Signs:
- Phishing scams try to trick people into revealing passwords, banking credentials, OTPs, or credit card information.
- Phishing messages may contain suspicious or unfamiliar links.
- Messages may create a sense of urgency.
- A message may claim that an account will be blocked or suspended unless the recipient takes immediate action.

Advice:
- Verify account-related claims using official sources before sharing sensitive information.
- Be cautious when a message asks you to click an unfamiliar link.


Scam Type: Fake Job Scam

Warning Signs:
- Fake job scams may promise unusually high salaries for simple work.
- They may ask applicants to pay registration fees.
- They may ask applicants to pay training fees.
- They may ask applicants to pay security deposits.
- Requests for payment during recruitment are a warning sign.

Advice:
- A legitimate employer generally does not require applicants to pay money in order to receive a job offer.
- Treat requests for payment during recruitment as a warning sign.


Scam Type: Investment Scam

Warning Signs:
- Investment scams may promise guaranteed returns.
- They may promise unusually high returns with little or no risk.
- Scammers may pressure people to invest quickly.
- They may ask people to transfer money to personal accounts.

Advice:
- Verify financial claims using official sources before sending money.
- Be cautious of guaranteed or unusually high returns with little or no risk.


Scam Type: Impersonation Scam

Warning Signs:
- Someone pretends to be a bank employee, government official, police officer, or company representative.
- The person claims there is an urgent problem with your account or identity.
- The person asks for passwords, OTPs, banking credentials, or other sensitive information.
- The person pressures you to act immediately without independently verifying their identity.

Advice:
- Contact the organization using its official contact details to verify the person's identity.
- Never share passwords, OTPs, or banking credentials with someone claiming to represent an organization.
- Do not make payments based solely on an unverified person's claims.


Scam Type: Payment Fraud

Warning Signs:
- A buyer sends a payment screenshot but the money does not appear in your account.
- Someone claims a payment is complete and pressures you to release goods before verifying receipt.
- Someone asks you to scan a QR code or approve a payment request to receive money.
- Payment details or transaction confirmations appear inconsistent or cannot be verified.

Advice:
- Check your bank account or official payment application to confirm that money has actually arrived.
- Do not rely solely on screenshots or messages as proof of payment.
- Never approve a payment request or share sensitive banking information without understanding what you are authorizing.


Scam Type: Romance Scam

Warning Signs:
- Someone you met online quickly develops a romantic relationship and asks for money.
- The person requests money for an emergency, medical treatment, travel, or another urgent expense.
- The person repeatedly requests financial help but avoids reasonable attempts to verify their identity.
- The person pressures you to send money or keep the relationship and financial requests secret.

Advice:
- Verify the person's identity and be cautious when an online relationship develops unusually quickly.
- Do not send money to someone whose identity or circumstances you cannot independently verify.
- Talk to someone you trust before responding to urgent financial requests from an online romantic partner.
